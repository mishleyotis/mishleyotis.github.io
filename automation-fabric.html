The scheduling bot

Workload allocation, in the place the team already is.

Allocating analyst workload across four time zones meant reconciling availability by hand, in threads, before anything could start. The bot moved that into Slack: request, allocate, confirm, without leaving the channel.

Deployed on Google Cloud Platform. Requests start in Slack because that is where the decision was already being made. Behind the conversation sits a unified intake dashboard, so every request and its owner are visible in one place. The bot assigns the work automatically and tells the Account Executive when it is done.

Before
Availability reconciled by hand, across threads, before work could be assigned
After
Requested in the channel, assigned automatically, tracked on one intake dashboard, and the Account Executive notified on completion
Built with
Slack · Python · Google Cloud Platform

The workflows

Five workflows that removed manual data collection.

Built in n8n and Zapier. Each one replaced a recurring task somebody was doing by hand, which is the only reason any of them exist.

Intake

Collection without a chaser

Inputs arrive, get validated, and land in the right place. Nobody has to ask twice for the same file.

Routing

The right person, automatically

Work routes on its own attributes instead of on whoever happened to notice it first.

Reporting

Status without a status meeting

Scheduled digests replace the weekly reconstruction of what actually happened.

Adoption

A workflow nobody trusts gets worked around.

And a worked-around workflow is worse than no workflow, because now there are two processes and only one of them is visible. So the build is only half of this project.

I am the team's single point of contact for artificial intelligence tooling over Slack: access requests, platform troubleshooting, and prompt-engineering and skill-usage support for distributed analysts. That is how I find out which parts of what I built people are quietly avoiding.

Support is a feedback channel

Every access request and troubleshooting thread is a signal about where the tooling is unclear. Answering them myself keeps that signal from getting summarised away.

Training is part of shipping

The same pattern from EY, where I delivered pipeline automation training to four country teams. A tool nobody was taught is a tool nobody uses.
