Overview
Static walkthrough · no scripts

Scores, peer benchmarks, and the narrative an account executive can read aloud.

01

Scores and peer benchmarks

Overall maturity to one decimal place, then the four pillar scores as a bar strip, each with its peer median and the cohort that median is drawn from. Every figure on the page has to equal the value actually served for that cell, and the headline ring and the run row must show the same number, because they are two views of one value.

02

Firmographics

Employees, revenue, assets, growth rate, headquarters, branches, founding year, primary regulator, charter. Each populated field shows where it came from, and an unknown field renders a dash rather than a guess. Figures must belong to this legal entity: a parent, a subsidiary, or a same-name institution is treated as contamination and the panel is withheld.

03

Why-now signals

Three to six dated external events, each with its kind, a one-line body, and its evidence identifier. A leadership change, an earnings move, a regulatory action, a technology announcement, an acquisition. A signal has to be an event, not a score read back in different words.

04

Executive summary

Four short paragraphs in situation, complication, question, answer order. At most one raw score in the whole summary: the story carries the argument, not the arithmetic. Every factual claim carries its evidence identifier, and client facts have to outnumber score references.

05

Opportunity tiles

Five tiles naming where value is available, each with a headline, a one-line rationale, and the capability it anchors to, written in words a client would recognise rather than internal capability codes.

06

Top findings

Four to six findings, each headlined by a capability and bodied as what, why, and so what, with evidence chips attached. The score shown and the capability named must refer to the same cell.

07

Leadership panel

The executives who matter to this particular conversation: name, title, tenure signal, and what they own that the assessment touches. An empty roster has to be recorded as verified absent rather than left silently blank.

08

Financial trajectory and sentiment

A dated multi-year series with the metric named, and a trend word only when the series supports one. Sentiment appears as source-level rating bars grouped by audience, each naming its source, rating, and sample size, so a thin picture declares itself as thin.
